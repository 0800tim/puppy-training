# Content review: the Tips tab, "and the other pets" (commit 9bf5d5a)

Reviewed 20 October 2026. Scope: the seven sections in `content.pets` in
`public/content.js`, shipped live on 18 October from Tim's brief about his
6-year-old Jack Russell and his partner's cat. Checked against SPCA New
Zealand, the AVSAB position statements and veterinary behaviour sources,
with links. Proposed rewording is in this document only; the app is
unchanged. Tim decides.

Overall: the section is reward-based, puts management first, and matches
SPCA NZ on the big points (growling is information, never let the dog chase
the cat, feed apart, scent before sight, the cat sets the pace). Four lines
lean on a correction or a startle, five state a number or a mechanism more
firmly than any source does, and two refer to a note on the chart that the
app cannot take. Nothing is unsafe as written; the rewordings make it
honest about what is known.

## Sources

- SPCA New Zealand, "How to introduce your new puppy to your dog" (Ask a
  behaviourist): https://spca.nz/advice-and-welfare/article/how-to-introduce-your-new-puppy-to-your-dog.
  Teach the puppy calm manners first; walk both on lead and treat the puppy
  for coming to you; "a low growl is an appropriate reprimand"; "you must
  never let interactions continue to the point of snapping or attempting to
  bite"; if only the puppy starts play, that is not appropriate; stiffness,
  lip licking or whites of the eyes mean stop.
- SPCA New Zealand, "Introducing a new furry family member":
  https://www.spca.nz/news-and-events/news-article/introducing-a-new-furry-family-member.
  Dogs: controlled meeting before the move, neutral fenced area on leads,
  parallel walking, brief sniffs, intervene in snapping "using something like
  a cushion, or a distraction such as a water pistol or hose", separate and
  consult a trainer or vet; toys only once they get along; bowls apart,
  supervised. Cats: scent swapping, feeding either side of a closed door,
  dog on lead in a sit or down, a second person rewarding the cat, short
  sessions, never let the dog chase ("play can quickly turn into hunting"),
  always an escape route and height, "a dog can seriously injure or kill a
  cat, even if they are only playing", keep apart when not home, some dogs
  are never safe alone with cats.
- AVSAB, Humane Dog Training Position Statement (2021):
  https://avsab.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/AVSAB-Humane-Dog-Training-Position-Statement-2021.pdf.
  Aversive methods "rely on application of force, pain, or emotional or
  physical discomfort" and "should not be used in canine training or for the
  treatment of behavioral disorders"; reward-based methods "should be used
  for teaching common training skills as well as to address unwanted
  behaviors"; management means "avoiding situations that lead to the
  unwanted behavior and ensuring the safety of all involved"; aggression
  "require[s] a treatment plan that includes environmental management" from
  a veterinarian or behaviour professional; avoid "shouting, staring, or
  forceful manipulation" and flooding.
- AVSAB, Puppy Socialization Position Statement (already in the app's
  reading list): https://avsab.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Puppy-Socialization-Position-Statement-FINAL.pdf.
  Socialise before the vaccine course is complete, with known healthy dogs.
- Animal Humane Society, "Safe group play for dogs and puppies":
  https://www.animalhumanesociety.org/resource/safe-group-play-dogs-and-puppies
  (read through a search excerpt; the page refuses automated fetches).
  Healthy play: play bows, bouncy movement, role reversal, "play freeze:
  dogs who play, pause, then resume"; puppies "are allowed to do (almost)
  anything with minimal consequence" while adolescents provoke older dogs;
  "let the older dog respond with a warning growl"; intervene "if the
  younger dog does not heed the warning or if the mature dog allows but is
  not enjoying the interaction"; mature dogs (2 to 3 years and older) may
  show little interest in play.
- AKC, "Are they playing or fighting?":
  https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/training/are-they-playing-or-fighting/.
  Soft mouths, role swaps, loose bodies, play bow on one side; stiffness,
  raised hackles, pinned ears, one dog chasing a dog that wants out on the
  other. "Do not attempt to separate the dogs by grabbing a collar or using
  any part of your body"; use "something big and flat".
- Byosiere, Espinosa and Smuts 2016, play bows in adult dogs (Behavioural
  Processes): play bows mostly follow a brief pause and restart play, which
  is the pause-and-resume pattern the app describes.

## Findings, section by section

Severity: **reword** (says more than the sources, or leans on a correction),
**note** (fine, add a qualifier), **ok**.

### Introducing her to the dog you already have

| Line | Verdict | Why, and proposed wording |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scent swap, neutral ground, parallel walk, three-second sniffs, older dog in first, toys and bowls up | ok | SPCA NZ's dog-to-dog steps, nearly point for point. |
| "A dog that can leave rarely needs to fight." | ok | AHS and SPCA both make the exit the first safeguard. |

### Two dogs in the house: the first months

| Line | Verdict | Why, and proposed wording |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Up to about 16 to 20 weeks most adult dogs give puppies a licence" | reword | The licence idea is well supported (AHS: puppies "allowed to do (almost) anything"; adolescents provoke) but no source gives 16 to 20 weeks. Propose: "For the first few months most adult dogs give puppies a licence ... Somewhere around four to six months the licence runs out". |

### If the older dog is a terrier

| Line | Verdict | Why, and proposed wording |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "If the terrier corrects her with a growl or an air snap and she backs off, that is a good correction. Say nothing." | **reword** | SPCA NZ draws the line before the snap: "never let interactions continue to the point of snapping or attempting to bite". Our line approves an air snap. Propose: "A growl that makes her back off is a fair warning: say nothing, then give them both a break. A snap, even one that misses, means it went a step too far. Separate for a while and set the next session up easier (more distance, puppy on lead, nothing to argue over)." |
| "A terrier that chases a squealing puppy can tip from play into prey in a second" | note | Prey drive in terriers is real and SPCA says play "can quickly turn into hunting" (about cats). "In a second" is rhetoric. Propose: "can tip from play into prey, and a puppy that chases a terrier will be told off hard." |
| "Guarding shows as a stiff body, a hard stare and a low growl ... Call the puppy away and reward her. Never take the thing off the terrier in front of her." | ok | Management and reward, matches AVSAB. |
| "Don't pick up the terrier to keep them safe from her. Being carried above the puppy makes guarding worse." | reword | Common trainer advice, no source found. Propose: "Picking the terrier up to keep them safe teaches the puppy to jump at your arms and often winds the terrier up more. Gate the puppy instead." |
| "Don't laugh off the terrier guarding the kids. A dog who guards a child from the puppy is a dog who may one day guard the child from a friend." | reword | Speculative chain. AVSAB: aggression needs a plan from a vet or behaviour professional. Propose: "Don't laugh off the terrier guarding a child from the puppy. Guarding people is a job for a reward-based trainer or your vet, early, while it is small." |

### Play or fight? How to read it

| Line | Verdict | Why, and proposed wording |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Green list (play bow, bounce, role swap, breaks, self-handicapping, soft mouths) | ok | AHS, AKC and the play-bow study. |
| Red list (stiff, hard stare, pinning without release, one dog leaving, escalating yelps, no breaks) | ok | AHS and AKC. |
| "The consent test: hold the more excited dog gently by the chest for five seconds and let go of the other one" | note | The idea (does the quieter dog come back?) is standard in trainer guidance and AHS puts it as "both dogs engage willingly, neither tries to flee". The exact form and "five seconds" are convention. Propose adding "This is a trainer's habit, not science, but it answers the question fast." |
| "Call breaks yourself every minute or two while she is small" | ok | AHS: interrupt and resume; play freeze is healthy. |
| "Match sizes: a tired puppy plays badly." | note | Unclear phrase. Propose: "A tired puppy plays badly. Nap first, play later." and drop "Match sizes". |

### When to step in and separate

| Line | Verdict | Why, and proposed wording |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "in different rooms for an hour", "An hour apart" | note | No source gives a time; it is a sane default. Propose "for a good while (an hour is plenty)" in both places. |
| "Biting is too much when it is teeth on skin with pressure, when it keeps going after the other dog has stopped, or when either dog yelps and the other does not back off" | ok | Matches AKC's hard-bite and no-release signs and SPCA's "never to the point of snapping". |
| "Split with your voice, a treat scatter or a door, never your hands. If you must, lift the back legs of one dog like a wheelbarrow and back away." | **reword** | AKC: never your body, use "something big and flat". SPCA NZ names a cushion, or a water pistol or hose as a distraction. The wheelbarrow lift is a known last resort for two people but puts hands near a fight. Propose: "Split with your voice, a treat scatter, a door, or something big and flat pushed between them (a cushion, a board, the baby gate), never your hands. The SPCA also suggests a squirt of water as a distraction. Wheelbarrowing a dog by the back legs is a two-person last resort." |
| "Write down what happened on the chart. Patterns show in a week." | **reword** | The app has no incident log; the only free text is a health note. Propose: "Rate the bite meter for the day and note what came before. Patterns show in a week." Or build a one-line "incident" note (see For Tim). |
| "Don't punish either dog afterwards." | ok | AVSAB. |

### Introducing her to the cat

| Line | Verdict | Why, and proposed wording |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Height and exits, scent first, sight through a gate, lead in the room, off lead only when she can nap near the cat | ok | SPCA NZ's cat steps plus the closed-door feeding they recommend, which we could add. |
| "Let the cat hiss and swat. A swat on the nose from a cat with claws in is the fastest lesson there is." | **reword** | This recruits an aversive and risks an eye. Propose: "If the cat hisses or swats, the session has gone a step too far: end it kindly and go back to the gate for a few days. A cat's claws can take an eye, so never set a swat up on purpose." |

## Does the visitor card agree with the Tips?

The visitor card (`/api/visitor/<token>`) prints the cue words, the biting
SOS steps, the house rules, the kids' rules and the never-give list. The
biting steps there are the same `content.sos.steps` the Tips tab shows, so
they agree. The card says nothing about a second dog or a cat, so a sitter
in a two-dog or dog-and-cat house gets no guidance on the two rules that
matter most for them: feed apart and never leave the puppy alone with the
older dog or the cat. Propose two lines on the card under House rules when
the family has other pets (the app does not know that yet; a Settings tick
"we have another dog" and "we have a cat" would let the card and the Tips
adapt). For Tim.
